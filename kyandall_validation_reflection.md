# MiniProject3 Validation Reflection (kyandall)

Before discussion, agreement on jbronyah's projects was high for the categorical fields. HasRecovered, ActivityPattern and Currentstatus were each at 100% (κ = 1.0), and RecentThemes was also at 100%. WhyRecovered was the outlier at 10%, with 9 of 10 projects in disagreement. After discussion all disagreements were resolved, and every field reached 100%. The high categorical agreement needs a caveat, though. My first-pass labels were checked against the author's summaries rather than re-derived independently. When I later inspected the raw commit histories, several labels looked weaker than the summaries suggested. Two examples: Smilei and PEcAn are labeled "sporadic" even though neither has had a zero-commit month in years. The main lesson for me is that agreement measured against a summary can overstate reliability.

Across both sets of projects, the clearest signal before a long gap was a sustained fall in annual commits, concentrated in one or two maintainers. TopicModelsVB.jl fell from 391 commits in 2020 to 10 in 2023 before its 23-month gap. MIRTK fell from 1,438 in 2016 to 6 in 2022 before its 29-month gap. rptR fell from 238 in 2016 to single digits before its 36-month gap. These projects didn't stop suddenly; the gap was the end point of a slow decline. A related signal was short pauses becoming longer. TopicModelsVB recorded seven gaps of three months or more before its longest one.

When a project did recover, the trigger was usually something outside it breaking, not a burst of new features. 3D-Wind-Field came back to adapt to CesiumJS 1.125 and WebGL2. wwt-web-client's restart included a CI update forced by GitHub retiring the Ubuntu 20.04 image. pyinterp-feedstock resumed packaging for a new upstream release. geoportal restarted on issue-tracked fixes and a Python 3.8 to 3.10 upgrade. Even when a reason was visible, the history rarely said why the maintainers came back. That's why we adjudicated WhyRecovered toward cautious wording, such as "recovery cause not established," instead of a generic category the evidence couldn't support.

Gaps didn't mean a project had died in several cases. GPUMD and mimix had one-month pauses followed by hundreds or thousands of commits. Some long gaps turned out to be data artifacts: in scRNA-seq_online, the "14-month gap" is just the time between one stray 2017 commit and the repository's initial commit in 2019. Some projects with no recent commits were stable rather than abandoned. Their last activity was dependency or documentation maintenance, and they still drew outside contributors. OpenSarToolkit, for example, received a pull request fixing the Windows gpt.exe path after its gap.

We hit four recurring interpretation pitfalls:

- **Dates after the snapshot.** Last-commit dates taken from the live repository can fall after the 2025-09-30 snapshot, which can flip Currentstatus. We fixed this by applying the cutoff explicitly.
- **Terminology.** "Yes, but limited" versus "Yes", and "No" versus "N/A" for projects with no gap. We agreed that limited recovery still counts as Yes and that N/A means there was no recovery episode, not a failed one.
- **Theme coding.** Keyword classifiers missed bot authors such as regro-cf-autotick-bot, and they read "fixed typo" in tutorial repositories as a bug fix. Coding one primary theme per commit, with bot authorship taking priority, made the labels comparable.
- **Sampling.** All-branch WoC exports and default-branch GitHub histories contain different recent commits. Several RecentThemes disagreements came from sampling rather than judgment, so we resolved them by agreeing on which commit population the field describes.
